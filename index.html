THE TECHNIQUE

Repoussé — worked entirely from the back.

Repoussé is an ancient metalworking technique, worked entirely from the reverse side. A hammer and a family of round-tipped tools raise the relief from behind, while finer tools define the detail on the front. Nothing is removed, nothing is added — the image already exists in the flat sheet, waiting to be pushed forward.

In Mexico, this technique has long given life to devotional art: sacred hearts, milagros and ex-votos worked in pewter. Alu Romero's atelier carries that heritage into a contemporary register — the same hand tools, turned toward the female body, flowers and everyday forms, then mounted on reclaimed wood, a frame or a mirror.

THE ARTIST

Mexican hands, Montréal light.

Alu Romero is a Mexican artist living and working in Montréal. Her practice explores the poetry of metal through hand-embossed pewter — a slow, entirely manual technique she carries from the Mexican devotional tradition into large-scale contemporary pieces for the wall.

Her work moves between several registers: sacred hearts and flowers with the directness of folk art, quietly sensual studies of the body, portraits and intimate scenes — all raised from the same flat sheet of pewter, by hand, from the back.

“Mexican artist in Montréal. The poetry of metal through pewter.”
